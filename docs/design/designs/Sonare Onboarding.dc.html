1Onboarding and the microphone check — six steps, then eight states

Demonstrate, then request: the learner hears a phrase and sees what syllable-level scoring looks like before being asked for a language, a name or a permission. The name is optional and says so. The permission is explained before the browser prompt fires, because on iOS that prompt happens once — a denial cannot be re-asked from the page, and recovery is a walk through Settings. Then a check that ends in a plain verdict, carried forward into the first activity. Nothing here is gamified: no streak, no count, no progress bar implying a course that has not started. Screens are 360×760 unless labelled.

Step 4 · the ask, explained before the prompt fires
1dThe load-bearing screen. What the microphone is for, what happens to a recording — stated so it is true and checkable, not reassuring — and the warning that the browser asks once.
Step 4 of 5

Next, your microphone

Scoring a sound means hearing you make it. There is no way around the microphone, so here is exactly what happens.

  • 1Each take is recorded on this device and uploaded to be scored. Nothing is uploaded until you speak.
  • 2Takes are kept for 90 days and then deleted automatically. Your sound history — the numbers, not the audio — is yours and is never expired on a timer.
  • 3When diagnostics are switched on by whoever runs this service, a copy of a recording can also be written to their disk. So we will not tell you nothing is ever stored.
  • 4You can export everything, or erase all of it, from the You tab — and see the counts of what was deleted.

Your browser will ask once

If you say no, this page cannot ask again — turning it back on means a trip through your device settings. Say yes when the prompt appears, then say anything you like to check it works.

Microphone blocked

The microphone was turned down

Safari only asks once, so we cannot put the question back up. Turning it on takes four taps in Settings.

  1. Open Settings on your phone.
  2. Scroll to Safari, then Microphone.
  3. Set it to Ask or Allow.
  4. Come back here and tap the button below.

On Android Chrome instead

Tap the padlock beside the address, then Permissions, then allow the microphone. Chrome will ask again after that.

If you would rather not, that is a fine answer. The listening activities need no microphone, and everything else stays available.
