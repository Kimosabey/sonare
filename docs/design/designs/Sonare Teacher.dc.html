1Teacher view, end to end — group sound difficulty and attendance, and no route to a per-pupil ranking

The plan says design this last, and says why: a class view is an authority figure holding a number about a child produced by a scorer whose fairness across accents is unmeasured. So the design constraint is stronger than “no leaderboard” — no pronunciation figure about an identified pupil appears anywhere in this view, which means there is no column to sort, no average to compute, and no ranking to build out of what the teacher is given. What a teacher gets instead is the thing they can actually act on in a lesson: which sounds the group finds hard, and who is turning up. Pupils join by choice, see exactly what their teacher can see, and can leave. Desktop 1280 is the primary width; the pupil side is a phone.

Sonare for a class

What you will see, and what you will not

Sonare scores pronunciation syllable by syllable. Whether that scorer is fair across accents has not been measured — so it does not put a number about a named child in front of you. Everything below follows from that one sentence.

You will see
  • Which sounds the class finds hard, as a group
  • The words those sounds show up in
  • Who has practised, and when
  • Which lessons the class has been through
You will not see
  • Any pupil’s pronunciation score
  • A class average or a ranking
  • Recordings of a pupil’s voice
  • Anything a pupil has not chosen to join
Why not per pupil

A number attached to a child’s accent, shown to the adult who grades them, is the one use of this scorer that could do real harm — and the accent fairness needed to rule that out has not been measured. Group difficulty answers the teaching question anyway: what do I reteach on Monday.

If that measurement is ever done and comes back clean, this decision can be revisited. Until then it is not a missing feature, it is the design.

Ordering food · Lesson 2

Four activities, about five minutes. Targets the r and the an.

repeatJe voudrais un café
listenPick the meaning — no microphone needed
respondUn café, s’il vous plaît
recall“and a croissant” → say it in French

One activity needs no microphone, which is what makes this settable for homework on a bus or a shared room.

What this is not

It is a suggestion on their Today screen, not a lock. Pupils can do it late, do it partly, or move on from an activity after three tries — and you will see that they practised, never a mark for how it went.

You will be able to see

How many did it, and whether the r bucket shape moved afterwards. That is the feedback loop — the class’s, not a pupil’s.

Requests, and their answers

Export the class’s sound difficultyYes — a CSV of the group table
Export attendanceYes — days practised, per pupil
See a pupil’s pronunciation scoreNo — not stored against a class
Rank or sort the class by progressNo — nothing to rank on
Hear a pupil’s recordingNo — never leaves their record
Remove a pupil from the classYes — and they are told

Three of these are not switches an administrator can turn on. The class view is not given the data, so there is no setting that would reveal it — which is the difference between a policy and a design.
